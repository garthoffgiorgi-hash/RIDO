- The hero ride card, the fare/ETA chip, and the comparison bars map to `FareChip`, `Card`, and a
  `TierProgress`-adjacent bar component. Build them as primitives, not as one-off landing markup.

  Preserve that ordering in the rebuild.

## ⚠️ Contradictions to resolve before this ships

The page states **three different "driver keeps" percentages**, and none is labelled with what it
nets out:

| Where | Claim | What it appears to net out |
|---|---|---|
| Hero stat | "Drivers keep **87%** of the average fare" | Commission + the $50 flat fee, at $5,000/mo volume |
| Economics section | "$4,200 kept · driver keeps **84%**" on $5,000 of fares | Commission + flat fee + card processing (~2.9%) |
| The model itself | **88%** | Commission only: $1,000×20% + $2,000×12% + $2,000×8% = $600 on $5,000 |

Three problems, in order of severity:

1. **"87% of the average fare" is not a fare figure.** A monthly subscription fee can't be netted
   out of a single fare. Per-fare, a driver keeps 80% (tier 1) to 92% (tier 3).
2. **During the pilot the flat fee is $0** (ADR-0003), so the launch-accurate number is 88% — the
   page understates RIDO's own advantage while launching.
3. **84% and 87% contradict each other** on the same page with no stated basis for either.

The "$1,240 more per month" figure inherits the same problem: it's derived from the 84% number
against an incumbent at 36.8%. Recomputed from the actual model at $5,000/mo — driver keeps $4,400
— the advantage is **$1,150 against a 35% incumbent and $1,400 against 40%**.

**Root cause, and the fix:** no file governs "what percentage do we publish." Give it one home in
`docs/business/monetization.md` with a named figure per definition — *commission-only*, *net of
flat fee*, *net of processing* — pick which one marketing uses, and have every surface cite it.
This is exactly the drift ADR-0004 exists to stop, and it is currently live on the front page
making the fairness claim.
